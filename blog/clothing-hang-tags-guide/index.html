Hang Tag Guide

Clothing Hang Tags: The Complete Guide for Fashion Brands

Learn what to put on clothing hang tags, how to choose tag size, materials and finishes, and how hang tags fit into a complete fashion packaging system.

Luxsome Packaging26 August 202610 min read

A hang tag may be one of the smallest pieces in a fashion brand's packaging system, but it sits directly on the product. That makes it an important bridge between the garment and the wider brand experience.

A good tag should look intentional, communicate what matters and feel consistent with the box, tissue, cards and other packaging around it.

What is a clothing hang tag?

A hang tag is a removable card or branded piece attached to a garment or accessory, usually with string, cord, ribbon or another suitable fastener. It can carry branding, product information and customer-facing details.

Unlike a sewn-in clothing label, the hang tag is part of the presentation the customer encounters before wearing the product.

What should you put on a clothing hang tag?

Start with the information the customer genuinely needs. Depending on the brand and product, that can include your logo, product name, size, collection information, care guidance, website, social handle, price or barcode.

You do not need to place everything on one side. A restrained front with the brand identity and a functional reverse often creates a cleaner result.

Hang tag vs clothing label

A clothing label is generally attached permanently to the garment and may contain size, composition or care information. A hang tag is removable and gives the brand more space for presentation, storytelling or retail information.

They should complement one another, but they do not need to duplicate every detail.

How do you choose the right tag size?

There is no single best hang-tag size. The correct dimensions depend on the garment, amount of copy, typography and desired visual impact.

A tag for a delicate scarf may need a different scale from one attached to a structured jacket. The tag should be noticeable without visually overpowering the product.

One-piece, two-piece or three-piece tags?

A one-piece tag is a strong choice when the information is concise. Two-piece and three-piece systems create additional surfaces for product details, care information or storytelling while keeping the main face clean.

More pieces are not automatically more premium. Use the number of layers that the content and brand experience actually require.

Paper, colour and finishing

Paper thickness, texture, edge quality and print execution all affect how the tag feels in the hand. Colour should connect with the rest of the packaging system, and finishes such as foil, embossing or debossing should be used deliberately.

A minimal tag on a beautiful stock can feel more sophisticated than a heavily decorated tag with competing effects.

How should a hang tag be attached?

The attachment should suit the garment and avoid damaging the product. String, cord and ribbon can each create a different impression. Placement also matters: the tag should feel integrated with the garment rather than randomly hooked onto it.

Do you need a barcode?

Not necessarily. If your inventory or retail workflow uses barcodes, the tag can provide a practical location for one. If you do not use a barcode-based system, there is no reason to add one purely for appearance.

Make the tag part of the packaging system

The strongest result comes when the hang tag looks related to the box, tissue, sticker, card and other pieces. Repeat selected typography, colours, materials or finishing details rather than designing the tag in isolation.

Frequently asked questions

What information should be on a clothing hang tag?

Common information includes the brand name or logo, product details, size, care guidance, website or social information where relevant. The exact content should support the product and brand rather than overcrowd the tag.

What size should a clothing hang tag be?

There is no universal size. The tag should suit the garment, amount of information, typography and attachment method without overwhelming the product.

Is a hang tag the same as a clothing label?

No. A hang tag is usually removable and attached externally, while a clothing label is normally sewn or otherwise permanently attached to the garment.

Do fashion brands need barcodes on hang tags?

Not every brand needs a barcode. Barcodes become useful when the brand's retail or inventory system requires them.
