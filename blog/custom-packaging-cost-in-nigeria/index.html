Pricing Guide

How Much Does Custom Packaging Cost in Nigeria?

Learn what determines custom packaging prices in Nigeria, including quantity, size, structure, materials, printing, finishing, inserts and sampling.

Luxsome Packaging26 August 202610 min read

One of the first questions brands ask when planning custom packaging is simple: How much will it cost?

There is no single price for custom packaging because the final cost depends on what is being made and how it is being produced. Understanding those variables makes it much easier to set a realistic packaging budget.

1. Quantity

Quantity is one of the biggest pricing factors. Custom production usually involves setup before the first finished piece is produced. Artwork preparation, cutting, printing setup, foil dies or structural development may be required whether you order a small batch or several hundred pieces.

As quantity increases, some setup costs are distributed across more units, which can reduce the effective cost per piece.

However, a lower unit cost is not useful if you order far more packaging than the business can realistically consume.

2. Size

Larger packaging generally uses more material. A rigid box developed for a folded abaya may require considerably more board and covering paper than a compact jewellery box.

Size can also affect cutting layouts, shipping, storage and the number of pieces that can be produced efficiently from a material sheet.

3. Box structure

Different structures have different material and assembly requirements. A magnetic flap box with concealed magnets, for example, is not produced in exactly the same way as a shoulder box or simple tray structure.

Choose the structure according to the product and the desired customer experience rather than simply selecting the most elaborate option.

4. Material

Speciality papers vary in colour, texture, finish, availability and cost. Some imported or specialised materials may cost more than standard alternatives.

The thickness of the board and any internal insert materials also contribute to the final quotation.

5. Printing and branding

A simple one-colour logo has different production requirements from complex artwork across multiple surfaces. Your artwork should therefore be designed with the intended production method in mind.

In many premium packaging systems, restraint is also an advantage: a well-positioned logo can look stronger than filling every panel with graphics.

6. Finishing

Foil stamping, embossing, debossing and selected gloss effects can add setup and production stages. Finishes are worthwhile when they strengthen the design, but they should not be added only because they are associated with luxury.

7. Inserts and internal components

A product that needs to sit in a specific position may require an insert. Jewellery, accessories, bottles, shoes and fashion items can all have different insert requirements.

An insert adds material and development work, but it can significantly improve protection and presentation.

8. The complete packaging system

Your packaging cost may include more than the box. A fashion system can contain a rigid box, branded tissue, hang tag, sticker seal, thank-you card, envelope, ribbon, care card or shopping bag.

You do not need every possible component. Decide what experience you want to create, establish a realistic budget and prioritise the pieces that contribute most to that experience.

9. Sampling

Custom packaging often benefits from a physical sample before the production run. Sampling allows the brand and producer to verify the physical interpretation of the specification, particularly when size, colour, structure and finishing are custom.

Why quotations can vary so much

Two brands can both ask for “100 luxury boxes” and receive very different quotations because their specifications may have little in common.

For a useful quotation, provide the product type, dimensions, quantity, colours, logo artwork, material preferences, finishing requirements and deadline where possible.

How should a growing brand set a packaging budget?

Start with the customer experience you need. If the budget is limited, prioritise a strong core system rather than spending heavily on one elaborate component and neglecting the rest.

A well-designed box, tissue, tag, card and sticker can create a cohesive experience. Additional elements can be introduced as the brand and order volume grow.

Use pricing as a planning tool

A quotation should help you understand the trade-offs between quantity, structure, material and finishing. The goal is not simply to find the cheapest box; it is to create packaging that is financially sensible for the business and appropriate for the brand.

Frequently asked questions

Questions brands ask us

Why is custom packaging more expensive at low quantities?

Setup and development costs are distributed across fewer pieces, so the effective cost per piece can be higher.

Does a bigger box always cost more?

Larger boxes generally require more material, but structure, material and production method also influence the final price.

Can I reduce cost without making the packaging look cheap?

Yes. Simplifying structure, limiting unnecessary finishes and focusing on a coordinated system can control cost while preserving a premium presentation.

Should I order a large quantity just to reduce the unit price?

Only when the quantity makes sense for your sales volume, storage and cash flow. Unused packaging inventory is still a cost.
