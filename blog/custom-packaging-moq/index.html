Custom Packaging MOQ: How Many Boxes Should Your Brand Order?

Understand custom packaging MOQ, why minimum quantities exist and how fashion brands can choose an order quantity that balances cost, storage and sales.

Luxsome Packaging26 August 20269 min read

MOQ is one of the first terms a growing brand encounters when it begins ordering custom packaging. It means minimum order quantity: the smallest quantity available for a particular production specification.

But the lowest MOQ is not automatically the best deal. The better question is: how much packaging can your brand use sensibly?

Why do packaging suppliers have MOQs?

Custom manufacturing involves work before the first finished piece is produced. Depending on the item, this may include artwork preparation, material sourcing, cutting setup, printing setup, dies, structural development and assembly preparation.

Many of those steps still exist whether the run is small or large, which is why extremely low quantities can be inefficient.

There is no universal packaging MOQ

A rigid box, hang tag, tissue sheet, sticker and thank-you card do not necessarily share the same minimum quantity. They use different materials, equipment and production processes.

Structure, size, finishing and custom tooling can also change what quantity is practical.

Why does the unit price change with quantity?

Setup and development costs are spread across the production run. When those costs are divided among fewer pieces, the effective cost per piece is usually higher.

That does not mean you should automatically order the largest quantity offered. Packaging that remains unused for months or years ties up cash and occupies storage space.

How many boxes should your brand order?

Start with your actual sales. Estimate how many relevant orders you fulfil each month and how many months of packaging stock you are comfortable holding.

Then consider whether the packaging design is likely to change. A fast-growing brand that expects to update its logo, sizing or product range may prefer a more conservative first run.

Remember storage

Rigid boxes can occupy substantial space when supplied assembled. Before increasing an order simply to reduce the unit cost, decide where the boxes will be stored and how they will be protected.

For suitable projects, a collapsible rigid structure can make storage more efficient.

Should every packaging component be ordered at the same quantity?

Not necessarily. A thank-you card may be used in every order, while a shopping bag may only be used for pickups or retail purchases. Consumption rates can differ across the packaging system.

Plan quantities around how each piece will actually be used.

Compare more than MOQ

When comparing quotations, look at the complete specification: dimensions, material, structure, printing, finishing, sample procedure and what is included. Two quotes for “100 boxes” can describe very different products.

Planning tip: choose the quantity that supports your sales, cash flow and storage—not simply the quantity with the cheapest unit price.

Frequently asked questions

What does MOQ mean in packaging?

MOQ means minimum order quantity: the smallest production quantity available for a particular packaging item or specification.

Why does custom packaging have a minimum order quantity?

Custom production can involve setup, sourcing, printing, cutting, tooling and assembly preparation, so extremely small quantities may not be economical to manufacture.

Is a lower MOQ always better?

No. The right quantity should balance total cost, unit cost, sales volume, storage capacity and cash flow.

Can boxes, tags and cards have different MOQs?

Yes. Different packaging pieces use different materials and production processes, so their minimum quantities can differ.
